# Real mission inspiration

OpenCareer can use documented aviation operations as inspiration for mission *patterns*. Generated player contracts remain fictional unless a future live-data feature explicitly identifies a factual source.

The point is realism: unusual aircraft are sometimes used for urgent work, military airlift carries people/equipment, and specialized aeromedical transport can create high-priority missions.

## Verified examples

### Donor heart carried by FB-111A — 1986

Official U.S. Air Force Global Strike Command history describes a February 14, 1986 mission in which an **FB-111A** from the 509th Bombardment Wing carried a donor heart from Oklahoma City to Hartford, Connecticut. The article says normal commercial/medical aircraft were not available that could meet the required timing, so the Air Force assisted.

OpenCareer pattern:

- extremely rare urgent organ-transport scenario;
- very short acceptance/dispatch window;
- medical/government priority;
- aircraft selection based on real capability/range/speed/access, not a hardcoded "fighter required" rule;
- exceptional pay/reputation can reflect urgency, but mission completion still needs actual flight evidence.

Do not describe this as a generic fighter-jet practice. The documented aircraft was an FB-111A and the mission was exceptional.

Source: U.S. Air Force Global Strike Command, "Whiteman remembers historic Valentine’s Day delivery" (2023), documenting the 1986 mission.

### C-17 troop/equipment airlift — 2014

A U.S. Army article documents C-17 flights carrying Rwandan soldiers and equipment from Kigali, Rwanda to Bangui, Central African Republic in support of an African Union effort. The article described a plan to transport roughly 850 soldiers and more than 1,000 tons of equipment.

OpenCareer pattern:

- military transport/troop-movement scenario;
- personnel + equipment payload constraints;
- staging-airport logistics;
- repeated rotations rather than a single generic "troop mission";
- conflict/security state can increase this job family without inventing a real operation.

Source: U.S. Army, "U.S. airlift Rwandans to Central African Republic" (Jan. 21, 2014).

### C-17 specialized ECMO evacuation — 2013

Official U.S. Air Force reporting documents a joint Army/Air Force team transporting a critically ill patient on ECMO from San Antonio to New York City aboard a C-17. The flight landed at John F. Kennedy International Airport before ground transport continued to the hospital.

OpenCareer pattern:

- high-priority aeromedical evacuation;
- specialist medical team/equipment requirements;
- strict time/reliability constraints;
- large-airport handoff/ground-transfer narrative;
- aircraft capability and access requirements remain authoritative.

Source: U.S. Air Force, "Medical team achieves cutting-edge patient transfer" (Feb. 6, 2013).

## Design rules

1. Preserve the operational lesson, not the names/identities of patients or participants.
2. Do not reproduce current tragedies as entertainment copy.
3. Current real-world conflict/security state, if enabled, requires current sourced validation and provenance.
4. Mission text may say that a scenario is "inspired by documented airlift practices," but generated contracts should not falsely claim to be a real military/government task.
5. Real company/agency names require sourced operational presence and should never imply endorsement of OpenCareer.
6. AI can narrate an already-selected scenario; it cannot choose authorization, rewards, aircraft compatibility or completion.
